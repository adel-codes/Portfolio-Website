The infusions

The doctor recommends an infusion, and its dose, after your assessment. Not sure which applies to you? That is what the consultation is for.

  • Hydration30 to 45 min

    Saline with electrolytes and magnesium, to support fluid and electrolyte balance where a doctor finds it suitable.

  • Vitamin C45 to 60 min

    Considered only after an individual medical assessment, with the dose set by the doctor. Not suitable in all circumstances.

  • Myers cocktail45 to 60 min

    Vitamin C, B-complex vitamins, magnesium and zinc, with doses tailored to your assessment.

  • Iron infusion1 to 4 hours

    For confirmed iron deficiency or iron deficiency anaemia. Vital signs are monitored throughout, and a small test dose may be given first.

  • Vitamin D injection15 to 20 min

    An intramuscular injection for confirmed vitamin D deficiency, dosed from your blood test results.

  • NAD+ infusion2 to 4 hours

    Given slowly, with the rate adjusted by the medical team throughout the session.

  • Antioxidant45 to 60 min

    A formulation of alpha-lipoic acid, vitamin C, glutathione and selenium, given under medical supervision.

IV infusions are health services delivered under the supervision of registered medical practitioners. They are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease, and results vary between individuals.

Before an iron infusion

Iron is given only when pathology confirms a deficiency. You will need results from the last 3 months that include:

  • Serum ferritin
  • Transferrin saturation
  • Full blood count
  • C-reactive protein (CRP)

A GP referral is also required. If you don't have recent results, the clinic can provide a pathology referral before your consultation. People with iron overload, haemochromatosis or a known sensitivity to iron preparations are not suitable. A follow-up blood test 6 to 8 weeks after the infusion confirms the response.

How it works

  1. Questionnaire

    Your history, medicines, allergies and goals, completed online. The $75 fee covers the doctor's review and your consultation.

  2. Assessment

    A doctor reviews your answers and talks with you about whether an infusion is suitable, and which.

  3. Pathology, where needed

    Iron and vitamin D need recent results. Others may, depending on your history.

  4. The infusion

    In the clinic, seated, with a cannula in your arm. Trained clinical staff, under a doctor's supervision.

  5. Review

    Follow-up and repeat pathology where the infusion calls for it.

On the day

  • Drink plenty of water in the hours before, and eat a light meal.
  • Wear clothing that leaves your arm easy to reach.
  • Bring a list of your medicines, supplements and allergies, and any recent blood results.
  • Allow 10 to 15 minutes in the clinic afterwards before you drive.

Safety and cost

Infusions given under medical supervision are generally well tolerated. As with any intravenous procedure, there can be bruising or discomfort where the cannula goes in, mild flushing or nausea, and rarely an allergic reaction. A full medical assessment comes first, and the clinical team is trained to manage reactions.

Most IV infusions are not covered by Medicare. Some private health insurers offer partial rebates under extras cover, so check with yours. The clinic provides receipts.

Questions people ask first

General information only. Your doctor will answer for your situation.

Do I need a referral?

Not for most infusions. All of them need a consultation with one of the clinic's doctors first. Iron infusions need a GP referral and recent pathology, and vitamin D injections need recent pathology.

How long does a session take?

Hydration and vitamin infusions usually take 30 to 60 minutes. Iron infusions take 1 to 4 hours depending on the dose, and NAD+ infusions 2 to 4 hours.

How often can I have an infusion?

It depends on the infusion and on your health. Some people have a single session; others follow a schedule. Your doctor recommends a frequency after your assessment.

I have a chronic condition. Can I still have IV therapy?

You're welcome to ask. Your doctor assesses suitability individually, and some conditions need extra clearance or rule out certain infusions. Disclose your full history, medicines and allergies in the questionnaire.

What's the difference from oral supplements?

Oral nutrients pass through the digestive system, where absorption varies. An infusion goes straight into the bloodstream. Whether that difference matters for you is a question for your doctor.

Start with the questionnaire. The doctor will say which infusion, if any, suits you.
