IV Therapy for Women in Brisbane: Fatigue, Iron, Menopause and Skin

Fatigue, low iron, perimenopause and skin are common reasons women ask about IV drips. Here is what the drips contain, and why a doctor checks your history and blood results before recommending one.

Women's nutritional needs change a good deal over a lifetime, from the reproductive years through perimenopause and beyond. Hormonal shifts, pregnancy, breastfeeding and long periods of high stress can all use up key nutrients faster than diet alone can replace them.

An IV drip delivers vitamins, minerals and amino acids straight into the bloodstream, bypassing the digestive system. Whether one suits you is a separate question. A registered doctor at the clinic assesses that individually. Any program is then designed around your health history and current symptoms, as well as what you want from treatment.

Fatigue and low iron

Fatigue is one of the most common complaints among women, and it often has more than one cause. Iron deficiency, B12 deficiency, low magnesium and ongoing stress are all common contributors. Where there is a genuine shortfall, a drip of B vitamins, magnesium, vitamin C and amino acids may help with several of these at once, supporting the cells' energy production.

Iron is handled separately. If you have confirmed iron deficiency or iron deficiency anaemia, an iron infusion is a medically supervised option your doctor may discuss with you, based on your pathology results and circumstances. The clinic needs pathology from the last 3 months (serum ferritin, transferrin saturation, full blood count and CRP) and a GP referral, and you have a follow-up blood test 6 to 8 weeks after the infusion.

Perimenopause and menopause

The hormonal changes of perimenopause and menopause ask a lot of the body. Many women feel tired and foggy through this stage, and mood changes and poor sleep are common too. Low nutrient levels are also common during the transition, and they can make all of this worse. Where a specific deficiency is found, a drip may be a useful complementary support. For the hormones themselves, the clinic offers personalised hormone assessment and management, and the two can be planned together.

Skin and recovery

Some women ask about drips for their skin. The clinic's Antioxidant Drip contains glutathione, an antioxidant the body makes naturally, along with vitamin C and B vitamins. Vitamin C also appears in some of the other formulations, and as a drip on its own. Whether any of these suits you is assessed individually by your doctor.

Others want a drip before a big event or after an illness. International travel is another reason. A drip replaces fluid and electrolytes quickly, and key nutrients with them. Many of the clinic's patients also book regular sessions as part of their routine.

Every treatment starts with a medical consultation, and your doctor recommends a formulation based on your assessment and, where relevant, your blood test results. Outside a confirmed deficiency, the evidence that most of these drips help is limited, which is part of why the assessment comes first.

Is IV therapy safe for women?

Given by qualified medical practitioners in a clinical setting, IV therapy is generally well tolerated. Every infusion at the clinic is supervised by one of its registered doctors, and every patient has a thorough medical assessment first.

Pregnancy is different. IV therapy is not appropriate during pregnancy without a specific medical indication and specialist guidance, and if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk to your treating doctor before considering any IV infusion.

Getting started

You start with an online health questionnaire. A doctor reads your answers and calls you within 1 to 2 business days, and nothing is prescribed from the questionnaire alone: the doctor goes through your history, your medicines and any pathology before recommending anything. Sometimes the answer is no.
