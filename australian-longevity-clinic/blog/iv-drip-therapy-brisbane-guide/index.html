IV Drip Therapy in Brisbane: What We Offer and Who It Suits

What's in each of the clinic's IV drips and what a session involves. Some uses have limited evidence behind them, and IV therapy isn't right for everyone.

At our Brisbane clinic, every treatment described here starts with a medical consultation and an individual assessment, and sometimes the answer is no. IV treatments are given under the direct supervision of our registered medical practitioners.

An intravenous (IV) drip delivers vitamins, minerals, amino acids and other nutrients straight into the bloodstream through a small cannula in a vein. Oral supplements have to pass through the digestive system; a drip bypasses the gut entirely. That may help people with digestive conditions that impair nutrient absorption.

The treatments

The drips come first. Iron and vitamin D are listed separately because they're only given for a deficiency that testing has confirmed.

  • Hydration Drip. Replenishes fluids and electrolytes. It's offered for recovery from illness, jet lag, heat exhaustion or general dehydration.
  • Myers Cocktail. Magnesium, calcium, B vitamins and vitamin C. Your doctor decides on an individual basis whether it suits you.
  • Vitamin C Drip. A vitamin C formulation, discussed only in the context of an individual medical assessment. This is general information: it doesn't describe a treatment outcome or replace medical advice.
  • NAD+ Infusion. Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) is a coenzyme found in every cell of the body, with a central role in energy metabolism and cellular repair. Levels decline naturally with age. IV supplementation is still being researched, and suitability is assessed individually.
  • Antioxidant Drip. A blend of glutathione, vitamin C and selenium, intended to neutralise free radicals and support cellular health. It's offered to people exposed to environmental toxins or high stress, and to those in intense physical training, but the evidence of benefit in these groups is limited.

Iron and vitamin D

Iron infusion. For patients with confirmed iron deficiency anaemia, IV iron delivers iron directly into the bloodstream, avoiding the gastrointestinal side effects often associated with oral iron supplements. Every iron infusion here is medically supervised and needs prior blood test confirmation of the deficiency: pathology from the last 3 months, plus a GP referral.

Vitamin D injection. Vitamin D deficiency is common in Australia, despite the sunny climate. For patients with clinically confirmed low levels, an intramuscular injection (into a muscle, not a vein) can replenish them.

How a first session works

Before anything else, you fill in our online health questionnaire and consultation booking form. Our doctors review your information and contact you within 1–2 business days to discuss your treatment options.

Your first visit to the clinic, at 628 Ipswich Road in Annerley, begins with a full medical consultation. The doctor reviews your health history and current medicines, along with your wellness goals, before recommending an IV formulation, if one is appropriate.

Once your treatment plan is confirmed, a registered nurse or doctor places a small cannula in a vein, usually in your arm, and connects a sterile bag holding the nutrient blend prescribed for you. It runs in slowly, typically over 30 to 60 minutes, and you can read or use your phone. Most patients feel no discomfort beyond the cannula going in.

Responses vary from person to person. If you notice any change in how you feel after an infusion, raise it with your treating doctor at your follow-up appointment.

Is IV drip therapy safe?

When it's given by qualified medical professionals, IV drip therapy is considered safe for most healthy adults. At the clinic, every treatment is prescribed and supervised by a registered medical practitioner, after a medical assessment that looks for contraindications. Infusions are given in a clinical setting with full emergency protocols in place, using appropriately regulated pharmaceutical-grade nutrients.

There are still risks, as with any medical procedure. You may get bruising at the cannula site, and allergic reactions, although rare, can happen. Treatments given without proper assessment can also cause electrolyte imbalances. That is why treatment is medically supervised, and why we don't offer IV therapy without a prior consultation.

Who it may suit

IV drip therapy may be appropriate for adults with nutritional deficiencies confirmed by blood testing, or with digestive conditions that impair nutrient absorption. Your doctor may also consider it if you're recovering from illness or surgery, or from intense physical training. The same applies if you have frequent headaches or migraines.

Persistent fatigue or low energy despite adequate sleep is a reason to be assessed first. Whether a drip has any part to play depends on what the assessment finds.

People also ask whether a drip will help during periods of high stress, or support immune function or skin health. Cognitive performance comes up too. The evidence for these uses is limited.

IV therapy isn't appropriate for everyone. Patients with certain heart conditions or kidney disease, or with specific medication interactions, may not be suitable candidates.
