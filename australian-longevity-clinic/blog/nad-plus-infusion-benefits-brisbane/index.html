NAD+ Infusion in Brisbane: Benefits, Evidence and What a Session Is Like

What a NAD+ infusion involves at the clinic in Annerley, and how far the evidence goes for the benefits people hope for.

Every living cell contains NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide), a coenzyme that takes part in hundreds of metabolic processes. Two matter most here: making ATP, the cell's energy, inside the mitochondria, and activating sirtuins, a family of proteins associated with DNA repair and cellular stress resistance, and studied for a possible link to longevity.

Levels fall naturally as we age. On current estimates, by our 50s NAD+ may be less than half of what it was in our 20s, and the decline has been linked to reduced mitochondrial function, impaired DNA repair, increased inflammation and many of the hallmark features of biological ageing.

What NAD+ does in the cell

Over the past decade, the link between NAD+ and ageing has been one of the most active areas of longevity research. Sirtuins need it as a cofactor. They help regulate inflammation and DNA repair, among other parts of cellular health, so when NAD+ falls, sirtuin activity falls too and cells are less able to repair damage and respond to stress.

NAD+ is also essential for the electron transport chain, the process mitochondria use to turn nutrients into ATP. Declining NAD+ is associated with mitochondrial dysfunction, which may show up as fatigue and cognitive fog, or as reduced physical performance.

PARP enzymes (poly ADP-ribose polymerases), which detect and repair breaks in DNA strands, need NAD+ too. Impaired DNA repair is a key driver of cellular ageing and cancer risk. Emerging research also suggests NAD+ plays a role in keeping circadian rhythms healthy, and those rhythms govern sleep quality and hormone secretion as well as metabolic function.

All of that is about what NAD+ does inside cells. Whether an infusion changes any of it in a measurable way, for a particular person, is much less certain.

Why a drip instead of a supplement?

NAD+ precursors such as nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) are sold as oral supplements, and in clinical trials they have raised NAD+ levels. An infusion puts the coenzyme itself straight into the bloodstream and reaches significantly higher peak plasma concentrations than oral supplements do.

That is why some people choose it when they want a faster or bigger rise, for example while recovering from illness or managing significant fatigue. Others use it as part of an active longevity protocol. IV is the more direct route, although a higher level in the blood has not been shown to mean a better result.

Who tends to ask about it

NAD+ infusions are commonly sought by people who:

  • have persistent fatigue or brain fog that other conditions do not explain
  • are following an active longevity or healthy ageing protocol
  • are recovering from a prolonged illness or post-viral fatigue
  • play high-performance sport or do demanding cognitive work
  • are in an addiction recovery support program
  • want to look after their mitochondrial function and cellular health

None of these on its own means an infusion is right for you. Every NAD+ infusion needs a medical consultation first to make sure it is appropriate for your circumstances, and the answer can be no. You begin by completing the clinic's online consultation form; the medical team reviews it and contacts you within 1–2 business days.

What people hope for

The benefits people hope for are more energy and less fatigue, clearer thinking, better sleep, and better mood and resilience to stress. Some have infusions alongside an addiction recovery program. The evidence for these benefits is limited, and responses vary from person to person.

The session itself

Infusions are given at the clinic, 628 Ipswich Road, Annerley, under medical supervision. They run slowly, typically over 2 to 4 hours, because giving NAD+ quickly can cause temporary nausea and chest tightness, or a flushing sensation.

The medical team sets the rate according to how you respond. A warm or flushing feeling is common, and if you have mild nausea or other discomfort, slowing the rate usually settles it quickly. Effects on energy, alertness, clarity or mood during and after an infusion vary from person to person.

The room is a comfortable clinical space. You are free to read or work on a laptop while the drip runs, or simply rest.
