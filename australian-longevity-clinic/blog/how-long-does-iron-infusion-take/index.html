How Long Does an Iron Infusion Take? Planning Your Visit in Brisbane

How to plan the day around an iron infusion at the clinic: the consultation first, then the infusion, then a short wait before you go.

It's one of the questions we're asked most often, and a practical one. You need to know how much of the day to set aside.

The infusion itself takes between 15 and 60 minutes, depending on the iron product used and the dose prescribed. Add a consultation beforehand and an observation period afterwards, and your total appointment time will typically be 60 to 90 minutes.

First, a consultation (15–20 minutes)

Every iron infusion at the clinic begins with a brief consultation with a doctor or nurse. They go through your blood test results confirming iron deficiency and check your medical history and current medicines for any contraindications. Then they confirm the right iron product and dose for you, and answer any questions you have about the procedure.

The infusion can't go ahead without those results. We need pathology from the last 3 months covering serum ferritin, transferrin saturation, a full blood count and C-reactive protein (CRP). If you're a new patient and haven't had a blood test, or your results are older than that, we'll arrange one before your infusion is scheduled. You'll also need a GP referral.

The infusion itself: 15 to 60 minutes

A small cannula goes into a vein in your arm, which takes less than a minute. The iron solution is then connected and given at a controlled rate. How long it runs depends on the product:

Iron productTypical infusion time
Ferric carboxymaltose (Ferinject)15–30 minutes
Iron polymaltose (Ferrum H)30–60 minutes
Low molecular weight iron dextran60 minutes

Ferric carboxymaltose (Ferinject) is one of the products we use. It can deliver a full therapeutic dose in a short infusion time, which makes it a practical option for many patients.

While it runs, you can relax, read, use your phone or work on a laptop. Most people find it comfortable and uneventful. Some notice a slight metallic taste during the infusion. That's normal, and it passes quickly.

Afterwards: observation (15–30 minutes)

Once the infusion is finished, we ask everyone to stay at the clinic for at least 15 minutes. It's a standard precaution. Delayed reactions to IV iron are rare, but they can occasionally happen, and this is when we watch for them.

Staff will check in with you and take the cannula out when you're ready to go. Most people feel completely well during this time and leave without any issues.

Before you come in

Eat a normal meal. You don't need to fast, and some people feel slightly lightheaded if they arrive hungry.

Wear something with sleeves that roll up easily, so the vein in your arm is easy to reach. Keep taking your regular medicines unless our doctor has told you otherwise. And bring your most recent blood test results if you have them.

Can I drive myself home?

Yes. There's no sedation or anaesthesia, so you can drive yourself to and from the clinic at 628 Ipswich Road, Annerley, and go straight back to work or your normal routine.

How soon will I feel better?

It varies. How quickly iron stores return to normal depends on how severe the deficiency was, and on other clinical factors.

We recommend a follow-up blood test 6 to 8 weeks after the infusion to confirm that your iron levels have been adequately restored.
