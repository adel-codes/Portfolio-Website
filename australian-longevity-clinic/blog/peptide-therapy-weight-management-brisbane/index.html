Peptide Therapy for Weight Management in Brisbane: How a Doctor-Led Program Works

Some peptides have been studied in relation to appetite regulation and growth hormone secretion, but none is a quick fix. This is how a doctor-led program works at our clinic in Annerley, Brisbane.

Weight that keeps creeping up, or won't come off, is frustrating. For many people it reflects hormonal and metabolic factors, along with neurological ones, that diet and exercise alone cannot fully address. Willpower is only part of it.

Peptide therapy is one medically supervised approach to supporting metabolic health. Research on certain peptides has looked at appetite regulation and growth hormone secretion.

At Australian Longevity Clinic in Annerley (628 Ipswich Road), every peptide program is individually designed and prescribed by one of our registered medical practitioners after a thorough assessment. We do not offer one-size-fits-all protocols.

Peptides commonly used for weight management

Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as biological signalling molecules, and different peptides interact with different receptors throughout the body.

CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin are prescribed together, as a combination of a growth hormone releasing hormone (GHRH) analogue and a growth hormone secretagogue. Growth hormone plays a role in metabolic function and body composition.

AOD-9604 is a modified fragment of human growth hormone, and Tesamorelin is a GHRH analogue. BPC-157 is also used.

Each of these has been the subject of scientific research, Tesamorelin in clinical study as well. The evidence for using them in weight management is still limited. No article can tell you which of them, if any, would suit you.

What a program involves

Before anything else, you fill in an online health questionnaire. A doctor reads your answers and calls you within 1 to 2 business days, and nothing is prescribed from the questionnaire alone.

A peptide program begins with a comprehensive medical consultation, in which your doctor reviews your health history, current medications, metabolic markers and weight management goals. Blood tests are typically required before you start. Depending on your individual situation, they may include fasting glucose, insulin, HbA1c, a lipid panel, thyroid function, sex hormones and IGF-1 levels.

Sometimes the answer is no. If peptides are appropriate, your doctor designs a personalised protocol. Most peptides used in weight management programs are given by subcutaneous injection, a small injection under the skin, typically in the abdomen. Our team provides thorough training so you can do this yourself, and ongoing monitoring is built into every program.

Realistic expectations

Peptide therapy is not a quick fix. It is meant to complement appropriate nutrition, regular physical activity, adequate sleep and stress management, not to replace them. Results vary between individuals depending on baseline metabolic health, adherence to the protocol, lifestyle factors and the specific peptides prescribed, and your doctor will set realistic expectations during your consultation and monitor your progress throughout the program.

Is it safe?

When prescribed and supervised by qualified medical practitioners, peptide therapy is generally well tolerated. The most common side effects are mild and localised: injection site reactions such as redness, swelling or bruising. Systemic side effects are uncommon when peptides are used at appropriate doses under medical supervision.

Most therapeutic peptides are Schedule 4 prescription medicines. Peptides should only be sourced through a compounding pharmacy with a valid prescription, and those bought online without one carry significant risks: they may be impure or contaminated, and the dose may not be accurate.
