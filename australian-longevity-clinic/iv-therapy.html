Overview

Intravenous therapy delivers fluids and nutrients directly into the bloodstream. It is a medical procedure, so we treat it as one. Before any infusion you see a doctor, who takes a history, reviews any recent pathology and decides with you whether an infusion is appropriate, and if so, which one.

Our range covers vitamin and mineral infusions, hydration, and iron infusions for people with diagnosed iron deficiency. The choice of infusion is a clinical decision rather than a menu selection, and we will tell you if we think an infusion is unlikely to help you.

Who it may suit

  • A documented nutrient deficiency that oral supplementation has not corrected, or cannot correct.
  • Diagnosed iron deficiency where a doctor has recommended intravenous iron.
  • Impaired absorption, for example after certain gastrointestinal conditions or surgery.
  • A broader program of care that one of our doctors is already overseeing.

An infusion is not appropriate for everyone. Some cardiac, renal and other conditions, certain medicines, and pregnancy may rule it out or call for a different approach. Your doctor will go through this with you.

What to expect

  1. Consultation and assessment. Your doctor takes a history, reviews your medicines and supplements, and orders pathology where it is needed to make a sound decision.
  2. Written plan and consent. You receive the proposed infusion, its purpose, known risks, alternatives and cost in writing, and your consent is documented before anything is prepared.
  3. The infusion. A small cannula is placed by clinical staff and the infusion runs under observation. Most infusions take between 30 and 90 minutes.
  4. Aftercare and review. You are given written aftercare advice and a clear point of contact. Follow-up is scheduled where your doctor considers it necessary.

Safety and eligibility

All infusions are prescribed by a doctor and administered by trained clinical staff, with monitoring throughout. Common effects include bruising or tenderness at the cannula site and a cool sensation in the arm. Less common effects are discussed with you beforehand.

Iron infusions carry specific risks, including skin staining at the site and, rarely, allergic reaction. For that reason they are only given after medical assessment, with appropriate precautions in place.

Please tell us

About every medicine and supplement you take, any allergies, any kidney or heart condition, and whether you are or may be pregnant. These details change what is safe.

Fees

Fees depend on the infusion prescribed and are confirmed in writing before you proceed. The initial consultation is charged separately from any infusion.

Where a Medicare or private health fund rebate may apply, reception will tell you before your appointment. Please ask for a current fee schedule at any time.

Questions

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly. If you have recent pathology or a letter from your GP, bring it along; it often saves repeating tests.

Can I have an infusion at my first visit?

Sometimes. Where your doctor's assessment on the day supports it and no further pathology is required, an infusion can be given at the same visit. Where results are needed first, we will book you back once they are in.

Can I drive afterwards?

Most people can. Your doctor will advise on the day if there is any reason you should not, and we recommend allowing some unhurried time after your appointment.
