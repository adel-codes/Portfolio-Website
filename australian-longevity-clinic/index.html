Better skin and hair

Skin and hair is one goal a peptide program can be built around. Recovery and sleep are others.

What a doctor may look at first

  1. Your history and medicinesAnything that would rule a peptide out
  2. Baseline bloodsHormone levels, inflammation and metabolic health, when your goal calls for them
  3. Iron studiesIf your hair is thinning, because low iron can be the cause

Before anything is prescribed

Most therapeutic peptides are Schedule 4 medicines. One is prescribed only if it suits you.

Then

Regular reviews of how you're responding. The program is adjusted, or stopped, from there.

Lose weight

Before recommending anything, a doctor goes through your weight history and the medicines you take, then the bloods below.

What a doctor may check

  1. Fasting glucoseYour blood sugar after an overnight fast
  2. InsulinHow hard your body is working to keep that sugar in range
  3. HbA1cYour average blood sugar over the last two to three months
  4. Lipid panelCholesterol and triglycerides
  5. Thyroid functionHow your thyroid is working

Before anything is prescribed

Prescription treatment only where it's clinically appropriate, alongside advice on food and activity.

Then

Regular reviews. Progress is read over months, not week to week.

Feel strong again

If low testosterone is why you feel flat, treatment aims to bring your energy and strength back.

What a doctor checks first

  1. Total testosteroneHow much is in your blood
  2. Free testosteroneThe part your body can use
  3. SHBGA protein that affects how much is free
  4. LH and FSHThe pituitary hormones that regulate production
  5. OestradiolYour balance of testosterone and oestrogen
  6. Full blood count and metabolic panelOverall health, and anything that rules treatment out

Before anything is prescribed

Only for men whose symptoms and blood tests both show low testosterone. Testosterone is a Schedule 4 medicine.

Then

Bloods again 6 to 12 weeks after starting, then every 3 to 6 months: testosterone, haematocrit, PSA and oestradiol.

More energy

Tiredness has plenty of causes. Low iron is one a blood test can find, and an infusion can replace.

What a doctor checks first

  1. Serum ferritinYour iron stores30–300 µg/L in women, 30–400 µg/L in men
  2. Transferrin saturationHow much iron is bound for transport20–50%
  3. Full blood countIncluding haemoglobinHaemoglobin 120–160 g/L in women, 130–170 g/L in men
  4. C-reactive proteinA marker of inflammation
  5. Vitamin DNeeded before a vitamin D injection

Typical adult ranges, from the clinic's guide to iron infusions. Labs vary slightly.

Before anything is prescribed

Iron is given only for a deficiency your bloods confirm, with results from the last 3 months and a GP referral.

Then

A follow-up blood test 6 to 8 weeks after an iron infusion.
