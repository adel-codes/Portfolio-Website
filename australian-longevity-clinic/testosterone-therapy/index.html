Before anything is prescribed

Only for men whose symptoms and blood tests both show low testosterone. Testosterone is a Schedule 4 medicine.

Then

Bloods again 6 to 12 weeks after starting, then every 3 to 6 months: testosterone, haematocrit, PSA and oestradiol.

What it is

Testosterone plays a part in muscle mass, bone density, energy, mood and libido. Levels fall with age. When they drop below the clinically normal range and cause significant symptoms, the doctor may consider testosterone replacement therapy.

At the clinic it is only ever a prescription medical service. That means an assessment first, pathology that confirms the deficiency, and a treatment plan made for you.

Symptoms that can go with low testosterone

  • Tiredness that doesn't lift, and low energy
  • Less muscle and more body fat
  • Lower libido, or reduced sexual function
  • Feeling low or irritable, or finding it hard to concentrate
  • Reduced bone density
  • Poor sleep
  • Less motivation and drive

These symptoms have many possible causes. They can't diagnose low testosterone on their own. A blood test and a medical assessment can.

How it works

  1. Consultation

    Your symptoms, history, medicines and general health, with one of the clinic's doctors. Everyone starts here.

  2. Pathology and diagnosis

    The doctor orders blood tests and reads them against your symptoms. Treatment needs a confirmed diagnosis.

  3. Prescription

    If it's clinically appropriate, a program for you. Whether that's an injection, a gel or another form depends on your needs and what you prefer.

  4. Monitoring and review

    Follow-up consultations and repeat pathology are how treatment stays safe, so they're built into the program.

Who it isn't for

It isn't for men whose testosterone is normal, it isn't for performance enhancement, and some medical conditions rule it out. Before anything is prescribed, the doctor goes through the options with you, along with the risks and benefits.

Questions people ask first

General information only. Your doctor will answer for your situation.

Is it suitable for everyone?

No. Only for men whose testosterone deficiency has been confirmed clinically, through pathology and a medical assessment.

How long does treatment last?

Usually it's ongoing rather than a short course. How long, and how often, depends on how you respond, your pathology results and your goals.

Are there risks or side effects?

Yes, as with any prescription medicine, and they vary from person to person. The doctor goes through them in detail at your consultation, and regular monitoring is how they're managed.

Do I need a referral?

Not to book the first consultation. Treatment itself needs a medical assessment and confirmed pathology.
