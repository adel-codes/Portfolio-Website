Overview

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. Some are well-established medicines. Many of those discussed in longevity circles are not approved products in Australia and are instead prepared as compounded medicines for an individual patient on a doctor's prescription. That distinction matters, and we explain it at the first consultation.

You will not find a list of specific peptides on this website. Prescription medicines cannot lawfully be advertised to the public in Australia and, more to the point, a medicine should be chosen for a person after assessment rather than before it. Your doctor will discuss specific options with you in consultation, including the evidence for each and where that evidence is thin.

Who it may suit

  • People exploring peptide therapies who want them considered within a medical framework, with assessment, monitoring and review.
  • People who have been offered peptides elsewhere and would like a doctor's independent view before proceeding.
  • People already using a prescribed peptide who want properly supervised, documented care.

A program is not offered where the assessment does not support it. Some peptides are prohibited in Australia and will not be prescribed under any circumstances. Your doctor will only prescribe within the law and within the evidence.

What to expect

  1. Consultation. An unhurried appointment to understand your history, your goals and what is realistic, and to go through the evidence relevant to you.
  2. Pathology where indicated. Baseline tests where they are needed to prescribe safely or to measure change.
  3. Written plan. What is proposed, its regulatory status, how it is administered, including training where self-administration is involved, the costs, and the points at which it will be reviewed.
  4. Dispensing on prescription. Medicines are dispensed on prescription in accordance with Therapeutic Goods Administration requirements.
  5. Scheduled review. Reviews at set intervals to assess effect and side effects, and to decide together whether to continue, change or stop.

Safety and eligibility

Compounded medicines have not been evaluated by the Therapeutic Goods Administration for safety, quality or efficacy in the way approved products have. Side effects vary by medicine. We discuss known and potential effects with you, document your consent, and review you on a schedule. Anything unexpected should be reported to the clinic promptly.

A note on sourcing

Peptides bought from unregulated online sellers may be mislabelled, contaminated or unsterile. If you have used such products, please tell your doctor. There is no judgement, and it affects your care.

Fees

Consultation fees apply. Program costs depend on the medicines prescribed and the review schedule, and are confirmed in writing before you commit.

Compounded medicines are not subsidised under the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme.

Questions

Are peptides legal in Australia?

Many can be lawfully prescribed by a doctor and prepared by a compounding pharmacy for an individual patient. Some are prohibited. Your doctor will only prescribe within the law and will explain the status of anything recommended to you.

Will you prescribe a specific peptide I ask for?

We will discuss it with you. Whether we prescribe depends on assessment, the evidence and your circumstances. We do not prescribe to a shopping list, and we will say plainly when the answer is no.

How long does a program run?

It varies with the medicine and the goal. Every program includes review points and an explicit decision to continue, change or stop. Open-ended prescribing is not our practice.
