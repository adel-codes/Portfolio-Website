Overview

Body weight is regulated by biology as much as by willpower, and medicine has more to offer than it did a decade ago. Prescription medicines can be an effective part of a program for some people. They are not for everyone, they have side effects, and they work best alongside changes that persist after the medicine stops.

We do not name specific medicines on this website. Prescription medicines cannot be advertised to the public in Australia, and your doctor will discuss what is suitable for you in consultation.

Who it may suit

  • Adults whose weight falls in the range where clinical guidelines support medical treatment, or who have weight-related health conditions.
  • People who have tried structured lifestyle change without a lasting result.
  • People who want medical supervision, with pathology and review, rather than a subscription.

Medicines are not suitable in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, and certain medical and family histories rule some options out. Your doctor will go through this with you before anything is prescribed.

What to expect

  1. Consultation. Your history, previous attempts, current medicines, sleep, and what a good outcome would look like for you.
  2. Assessment. Measurements and pathology, including metabolic markers, so that the plan rests on your results.
  3. Written plan. What is prescribed, if anything, alongside nutrition and activity guidance, sleep, and a review schedule.
  4. Review. Regular reviews, more frequent early on, to manage side effects, adjust doses and keep the plan realistic.
  5. Maintenance. Planning for the period after medicines, which is where many programs fail and where ours is designed to hold.

Safety and eligibility

Side effects of weight-management medicines are commonly gastrointestinal and often settle; less common but more serious effects are discussed with you beforehand. Supply of some medicines in this class has at times been constrained in Australia, and we will tell you if this affects your plan.

Not a quick fix

Weight regain is common when medicines stop without other change. The program is built around that fact from the first appointment, not as an afterthought.

Fees

Consultation fees apply. Medicines are dispensed by a pharmacy at their cost, and most are not subsidised under the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme for weight management.

Fees and likely medicine costs are set out in writing before you begin.

Questions

Will I have to inject?

It depends on the medicine. Some are oral, some are injected. Where self-injection is involved, you are trained in clinic and supported after.

What happens when I stop the medicine?

Some weight regain is common unless other changes have taken hold, which is why nutrition, movement and sleep are part of the program from the start, and why we plan the end of medicines with you rather than letting them lapse.

Do you offer this by telehealth?

The initial consultation and assessment are in clinic. Ask reception about review appointments; some can be arranged remotely once you are established in a program.
