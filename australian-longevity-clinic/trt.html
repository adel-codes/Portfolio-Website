Overview

Low testosterone can produce real symptoms. Those symptoms also overlap with poor sleep, depression, excess weight, medication effects and ordinary ageing. Treating a number without the right diagnosis is poor medicine, so the first job is to establish whether a deficiency exists and, if it does, why.

Where therapy is indicated, your doctor will discuss the options and how they are administered, the monitoring required, and the implications for fertility, which can be significant. Where therapy is not indicated, we will say so and talk through what else may help.

Who it may suit

  • Men with persistent symptoms and repeated low morning testosterone on pathology.
  • Men with a diagnosed condition affecting the testes or pituitary.
  • Men already on therapy who want supervised, properly monitored care.

Therapy is generally not suitable for men hoping to conceive in the near term without additional measures, men with certain prostate or breast cancers, untreated severe sleep apnoea or a very high red cell count. Your doctor will go through the contraindications with you.

What to expect

  1. Consultation and history. Symptoms, sleep, mood, medicines, lifestyle and family history, and what you hope treatment will change.
  2. Pathology. Two separate early-morning samples, together with the related tests needed to interpret them and to prescribe safely.
  3. Diagnosis and discussion. A plain explanation of what the results show, the options including not treating, and what each would involve.
  4. Prescription and administration plan. If therapy is indicated: the formulation, how it is administered, what it costs and how it will be monitored, in writing.
  5. Monitoring. Blood tests at intervals, review of symptoms and side effects, and prostate health checks as appropriate for your age and history.

Safety and eligibility

Testosterone therapy requires monitoring. Risks include a raised red cell count, acne, fluid retention, breast tenderness, reduced sperm production and effects on the prostate. Its cardiovascular effects continue to be studied. Monitoring is not optional with us, and we will not continue to prescribe without it.

PBS and specialist care

Subsidised testosterone under the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme requires specific criteria to be met, including specialist involvement. Where this may apply to you, we will say so and help arrange it.

Fees

Consultation fees apply and are confirmed at booking. Pathology is billed by the laboratory, and many tests attract a Medicare rebate.

Medicine costs depend on the formulation prescribed and whether PBS criteria are met. We set these out in writing before you begin.

Questions

Do you treat women?

This program is for men. Testosterone and hormonal health in women is a separate clinical question, and you are welcome to raise it at a general consultation.

How soon would I notice a change?

It varies. Some changes appear over weeks, others over months, and some men notice little, which is itself useful information for deciding whether to continue.

Can I stop once I have started?

Yes, with medical guidance. Stopping after a long period of therapy should be supervised, and we will plan it with you.
