Peptide programs, prescribed around one goal.

Peptides are short chains of amino acids that the body uses as signals. A program here is built around one goal of yours, and only after a doctor has been through your history and the medicines you take.

Whether a peptide suits your goal, and which one, is something you and the doctor decide together after your assessment.

You won't find peptides named on this site. Australian law doesn't allow prescription medicines to be advertised to the public, and the right one depends on you anyway.

How a program runs

  1. Consultation

    With one of the clinic's doctors: your goals, your history, the medicines you take, and anything that would rule a peptide out.

  2. Prescription and plan

    If a peptide suits you, the doctor prescribes it and sets the dose, the schedule and how long the program runs.

  3. Administration

    Most programs use a small injection under the skin. The clinic shows you how and gives you written instructions to take home.

  4. Monitoring and review

    Regular reviews of how you're responding. The program is adjusted, or stopped, from there.

Suitability and safety

Peptide programs aren't for everyone. They aren't recommended in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. If you have active cancer, an autoimmune disorder or significant organ impairment, the doctor needs to look at your situation carefully before anything is decided.

Side effects depend on the peptide. The common ones are mild: redness or soreness where the injection goes in, some tiredness, or fluid retention. Tell the doctor about every medicine and supplement you take, because each combination is assessed on its own.

People respond very differently. Some notice a change within weeks; for others it takes months. A program works alongside your sleep, nutrition and exercise.

Questions people ask first

General information only. Your doctor will answer for your situation.

Do I need a prescription?

Yes. Most therapeutic peptides are Schedule 4 prescription medicines in Australia, so you'll have a consultation before anything is prescribed.

How are peptides given?

It depends on the peptide and the program. Usually it's an injection under the skin; sometimes it's an injection into muscle, a nasal spray or something taken by mouth. The doctor tells you which, and shows you how.

Can a peptide program be combined with other treatments?

Often, for example with IV therapy or hormone treatment. Each combination is checked individually for interactions.

Is it covered by Medicare or private health insurance?

Generally not by Medicare. Ask your health fund about extras cover; the clinic gives you an itemised receipt.
